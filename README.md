# CS-230-Operating-Platforms
Portfolio work from CS 230 Operating Platforms

## Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?
The Gaming Room was the client, and they wanted to take their game, Draw It or Lose It, and expand it from Android to a web-based application that could work on different platforms. The application needed to keep track of games, teams, and players while making sure there were no duplicate names or IDs. They also wanted the game to work well for multiple users on different devices as more people started playing.

## What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?
I think one thing I did well was comparing the different operating platforms and figuring out which one would work best for The Gaming Room. Instead of just choosing a platform, I looked at the advantages and disadvantages of Windows, Mac, Linux, and mobile devices. This helped me understand why Linux would be a good choice for the server while still allowing players on other platforms to access the game.

## What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
Working through the design document helped me understand how the different parts of the application were supposed to work together before getting too far into the code. Having the requirements and UML diagram already planned out made it easier to see how the games, teams, and players were connected. I think having a plan like this also helps avoid confusion later because you have something to look back at while developing the application.

## If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
If I could revise one part of the design document, I would probably go back to the evaluation section. I think I could add more detail when comparing the different operating platforms and explain some of their differences better. Now that I have learned more about each platform throughout the course, I would have a better idea of what to look for and how those differences could affect the application.

## How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
I looked at what The Gaming Room wanted the application to do and used those requirements when making decisions about the design. Since they wanted the game to work on different platforms and support multiple users, I focused on creating a design that could grow while still keeping the game organized. I think considering the user's needs is important because the software might work correctly, but that does not mean much if it does not actually solve what the client needed in the first place.

## How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?
My approach was to first understand what the client needed and then plan out how the application should work. Using the requirements and UML diagram helped me organize everything before developing it. In the future, I would use a similar approach by planning ahead and making sure the design matches what the client needs.
